📊 How these work together: New inventory sits behind your existing stock — the LOC won't start paying down until current inventory sells through first.

⚖️ Demand plan context: When analyzing this stock-up investment, consider how much inventory you currently have compared to how much you want to have per your demand plan.

If you have less than your demand plan target: A portion of this stock-up is actually serving initial or normal demand — inventory you'd be buying anyway. This diminishes the true impact of the interest expense, since you'd be carrying this inventory regardless of the price increase decision.

If you have more than your demand plan target: You're already "over-stocked" on inventory. Adding more on top of that increases risk and it's important to understand our current stock position well. Consider what the impact of stacking on top of an existing surplus looks like before committing. Don't let this hold you back from a strategic stock-up, but make it a variable in your decision making process.

Keep this demand plan context in mind when making the final strategic decision — the calculator models the financial mechanics, but the demand picture is the business reality behind the numbers.
